INTRODUCTION

Understanding How Households Use Energy

Residential buildings account for approximately 20% of global energy consumption. Understanding household energy patterns is critical for demand response programs, energy efficiency initiatives, and grid infrastructure planning.

Dimensionality Reduction

PCA Reduction

14 raw features reduced to 3 principal components capturing 72% of total variance in consumption behavior.

Unsupervised Learning

K-Means Clustering

Optimal k=5 clusters identified via elbow method and silhouette analysis, revealing distinct consumption archetypes.

LIMITATIONS & FUTURE WORK

Constraints and Next Steps

Limitations

Single Household

Dataset from one residence — generalizability requires multi-household validation across regions and climates.

Temporal Scope

2006–2010 data — modern smart meter data may show different patterns with new appliance efficiencies.

No External Factors

Weather, occupancy, and appliance inventory not directly measured — potential confounders.

Static Clustering

K-Means assumes fixed archetypes — household behavior may evolve over time.

Future Directions

  • Validate on smart meter datasets (UK-PD, REFIT, GREEND)
  • Apply DBSCAN for density-based clustering to handle irregular shapes
  • Incorporate weather data as external features for seasonal adjustment
  • Time-series decomposition for intra-cluster temporal dynamics
  • Deep learning autoencoders for non-linear dimensionality reduction
  • Real-time clustering for live demand response systems
